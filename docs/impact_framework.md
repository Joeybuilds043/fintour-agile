# Horizon Tourism — Intended Development Impact

## Problem hypothesis
Tourism assets and local experiences may be difficult for visitors to discover and evaluate because information is fragmented. Destination managers and tourism enterprises may also lack comparable evidence for investment and stewardship decisions. These hypotheses require field validation.

## Intended beneficiaries
Visitors; community-owned accommodation and experience providers; local guides and artisans; destination management organisations; tourism entrepreneurs; public-sector stakeholders.

## Potential outcomes
- Improved visibility of lesser-known destinations and local providers.
- Better access to practical, culturally informed travel information.
- More informed destination investment and operating decisions.
- Better seasonal distribution and visitor management.
- Stronger local participation in tourism value chains.

## Indicators to test
Destination search task completion; visitor information trust; local supplier clicks or enquiries; itinerary completion; share of published claims with traceable sources; business participation; local procurement share where surveyed; visitor distribution by season where data exists.

## Attribution caution
A prototype cannot prove that it increases tourism arrivals, jobs or local income. Those outcomes require deployment, comparison data, longitudinal monitoring and stakeholder partnerships.
